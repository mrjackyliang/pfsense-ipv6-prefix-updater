Choose how often pfSense checks your prefix and whether you get a <Terminology title="alert sent after a verified successful update" to="/docs/reference/terminology#change-notification">change notification</Terminology> after a successful update.

## Choose a Schedule

| Schedule          | When it runs                               |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Every 15 minutes  | At minutes 0, 15, 30, and 45 of every hour |
| Hourly            | At minute 0 of every hour, the default     |
| Every 6 hours     | At 00:00, 06:00, 12:00, and 18:00          |
| Daily at midnight | At 00:00                                   |

The times follow the firewall's time zone, while the Status tab shows UTC.

## Turn On Scheduled Updates

**<Terminology title="automatic checks run from a pfSense-managed cron entry" to="/docs/reference/terminology#scheduled-updates">Enable Scheduled Updates</Terminology>** is off after installation. Saving Settings adds or removes a pfSense-managed cron entry, so no Cron package or hand-written cron job is needed.

With the box cleared, no check runs on its own, while **Run Now** and **<Terminology title="preview that never backs up or writes" to="/docs/reference/terminology#dry-run">Dry Run</Terminology>** keep working. It is recommended to turn it on only after the [first run](/docs/first-run) has recorded and verified the <Terminology title="starting prefix recorded without changing configuration" to="/docs/reference/terminology#baseline">baseline</Terminology>.

## What a Routine Check Costs

A <Terminology title="outcome: prefix unchanged, nothing scanned or written" to="/docs/reference/terminology#no-change">No change</Terminology> check reads only its settings, the interface addresses, and the <Terminology title="package file holding the baseline and last run" to="/docs/reference/terminology#package-state">package state</Terminology>. It doesn't scan the configuration, create a backup, reload services, or send a notification.

It still writes one line to the <Terminology title="pfSense general log the package writes to" to="/docs/reference/terminology#system-log">system log</Terminology>.

## Overlapping Runs

A <Terminology title="guard that stops two checks running at once" to="/docs/reference/terminology#run-lock">run lock</Terminology> lets only one check run at a time. A check that starts while another is running exits without reading the configuration and leaves **Last Run** unchanged.

That check logs `Another run owns the package lock; this invocation exited without reading configuration.` to the <Terminology title="pfSense general log the package writes to" to="/docs/reference/terminology#system-log">system log</Terminology>.

## Change Notifications

**<Terminology title="alert sent after a verified successful update" to="/docs/reference/terminology#change-notification">Send Change Notifications</Terminology>** is on by default. After a <Terminology title="outcome: fields updated, saved, reloaded, and verified" to="/docs/reference/terminology#success">Success</Terminology>, it sends a message through pfSense's <Terminology title="remote alert services configured in pfSense" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notification-channels">notification channels</Terminology> and adds a <Terminology title="alert shown in the pfSense notices list" to="/docs/reference/terminology#webgui-notice">WebGUI notice</Terminology>.

The message reads like `Updated 1 approved field from 2001:db8:abcd:7800::/56 to 2001:db8:abcd:8800::/56 on interface lan.` No other outcome sends a <Terminology title="alert sent after a verified successful update" to="/docs/reference/terminology#change-notification">change notification</Terminology>.

If delivery fails, the update still counts as a <Terminology title="outcome: fields updated, saved, reloaded, and verified" to="/docs/reference/terminology#success">Success</Terminology>. The Status tab doesn't show delivery status, and the <Terminology title="pfSense general log the package writes to" to="/docs/reference/terminology#system-log">system log</Terminology> records `Configuration succeeded, but the change notification failed.`

## Warning and Failure Notices

<Terminology title="outcome: prefix changed, but no approved field matched" to="/docs/reference/terminology#warning">Warning</Terminology> and <Terminology title="outcome: a check or safety stage stopped the run" to="/docs/reference/terminology#failure">Failure</Terminology> outcomes always add a local <Terminology title="alert shown in the pfSense notices list" to="/docs/reference/terminology#webgui-notice">WebGUI notice</Terminology>, even with **<Terminology title="alert sent after a verified successful update" to="/docs/reference/terminology#change-notification">Send Change Notifications</Terminology>** cleared. These notices aren't sent to <Terminology title="remote alert services configured in pfSense" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notification-channels">notification channels</Terminology>, and a cause that isn't fixed adds a new one on every scheduled run.

## Validate the Schedule

1. Save Settings and confirm the `Settings saved.` message.
2. On the Status tab, select **Run Now** and confirm a <Terminology title="outcome: prefix unchanged, nothing scanned or written" to="/docs/reference/terminology#no-change">No change</Terminology> outcome with a current **Timestamp (UTC)**.
3. Confirm that **<Terminology title="copy of the configuration saved before a write" to="/docs/reference/terminology#configuration-backup">Configuration Backup Created</Terminology>** shows No and **Changed Fields** shows `0`.
4. After the next scheduled time, refresh the Status tab and confirm that the timestamp moved.
5. To test delivery, use pfSense's own test buttons under **System → Advanced → Notifications**.

Continue with [Status and Actions](/docs/operate/status-and-actions) for the fields that scheduled checks and <Terminology title="check started from the Status tab" to="/docs/reference/terminology#manual-run">manual runs</Terminology> record.
