Choose how often pfSense checks your prefix and whether you get a <Terminology title="The alert sent after your addresses were updated" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">change notification</Terminology> after a successful update.

## Choose a Schedule

| Schedule          | When it runs                               |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Every 15 minutes  | At minutes 0, 15, 30, and 45 of every hour |
| Hourly            | At minute 0 of every hour, the default     |
| Every 6 hours     | At 00:00, 06:00, 12:00, and 18:00          |
| Daily at midnight | At 00:00                                   |

The times follow the firewall's time zone, while the Status tab shows UTC.

## Turn On Scheduled Updates

**<Terminology title="Automatic checks on a schedule, off until you enable them" to="/docs/reference/terminology#running-checks">Enable Scheduled Updates</Terminology>** is off after installation. Saving Settings adds or removes a pfSense-managed cron entry, so no Cron package or hand-written cron job is needed.

With the box cleared, no check runs on its own, while **Run Now** and **<Terminology title="A preview of the changes that writes nothing" to="/docs/reference/terminology#running-checks">Dry Run</Terminology>** keep working. It is recommended to turn it on only after the [first run](/docs/first-run) has recorded and verified the <Terminology title="The recorded starting prefix that changes are measured from" to="/docs/reference/terminology#baselines-and-transitions">baseline</Terminology>.

## What a Routine Check Costs

A <Terminology title="Same prefix as last time, so there is nothing to do" to="/docs/reference/terminology#run-outcomes">No change</Terminology> check reads only its settings, the interface addresses, and the <Terminology title="The file holding each baseline, kept across reinstalls" to="/docs/reference/terminology#running-checks">package state</Terminology>. It doesn't scan the configuration, create a backup, reload services, or send a notification.

It still writes one line to the <Terminology title="pfSense's general log, where every run leaves lines" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">system log</Terminology>.

## Overlapping Runs

A <Terminology title="Keeps two checks from ever running at once" to="/docs/reference/terminology#running-checks">run lock</Terminology> lets only one check run at a time. A check that starts while another is running exits without reading the configuration and leaves **Last Run** unchanged.

That check logs `Another run owns the package lock; this invocation exited without reading configuration.` to the <Terminology title="pfSense's general log, where every run leaves lines" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">system log</Terminology>.

## Change Notifications

**<Terminology title="The alert sent after your addresses were updated" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">Send Change Notifications</Terminology>** is on by default. After a <Terminology title="Prefix changed and every approved field was updated" to="/docs/reference/terminology#run-outcomes">Success</Terminology>, it sends a message through pfSense's <Terminology title="pfSense's email and other remote alert services" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">notification channels</Terminology> and adds a <Terminology title="An alert in pfSense's own notices list" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">WebGUI notice</Terminology>.

The message reads like `Updated 1 approved field from 2001:db8:abcd:7800::/56 to 2001:db8:abcd:8800::/56 on interface lan.` No other outcome sends a <Terminology title="The alert sent after your addresses were updated" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">change notification</Terminology>.

If delivery fails, the update still counts as a <Terminology title="Prefix changed and every approved field was updated" to="/docs/reference/terminology#run-outcomes">Success</Terminology>. The Status tab doesn't show delivery status, and the <Terminology title="pfSense's general log, where every run leaves lines" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">system log</Terminology> records `Configuration succeeded, but the change notification failed.`

## Warning and Failure Notices

<Terminology title="Prefix changed, but no approved field used the old one" to="/docs/reference/terminology#run-outcomes">Warning</Terminology> and <Terminology title="The run stopped at a check or safety stage" to="/docs/reference/terminology#run-outcomes">Failure</Terminology> outcomes always add a local <Terminology title="An alert in pfSense's own notices list" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">WebGUI notice</Terminology>, even with **<Terminology title="The alert sent after your addresses were updated" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">Send Change Notifications</Terminology>** cleared. These notices aren't sent to <Terminology title="pfSense's email and other remote alert services" to="/docs/reference/terminology#notifications-and-logs">notification channels</Terminology>, and a cause that isn't fixed adds a new one on every scheduled run.

## Validate the Schedule

1. Save Settings and confirm the `Settings saved.` message.
2. On the Status tab, select **Run Now** and confirm a <Terminology title="Same prefix as last time, so there is nothing to do" to="/docs/reference/terminology#run-outcomes">No change</Terminology> outcome with a current **Timestamp (UTC)**.
3. Confirm that **<Terminology title="A copy of the config taken right before any write" to="/docs/reference/terminology#backups-and-service-reloads">Configuration Backup Created</Terminology>** shows No and **Changed Fields** shows `0`.
4. After the next scheduled time, refresh the Status tab and confirm that the timestamp moved.
5. To test delivery, use pfSense's own test buttons under **System → Advanced → Notifications**.

Continue with [Status and Actions](/docs/operate/status-and-actions) for the fields that scheduled checks and <Terminology title="A check you start yourself, even with schedules off" to="/docs/reference/terminology#running-checks">manual runs</Terminology> record.
